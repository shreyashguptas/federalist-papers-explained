### A3. ⚠️ The publication date — a genuine source conflict, deliberately hedged

Sources disagree by one day:

- **Yale Avalon** header: "From the New York Packet. Friday, January 4, 1788."
- **Founders Online** (the scholarly Hamilton Papers edition): "The Federalist No. 34, **[5 January 1788]**."
  https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Hamilton/01-04-02-0191
- Wikipedia and several secondary sources: 5 January 1788.

Independent check: 4 January 1788 **was** a Friday (computed), so Avalon's internal
detail is self-consistent, and the New-York Packet was a Tuesday/Friday paper —
which makes a Saturday 5 January Packet issue doubtful. Against that, Founders
Online is the authoritative modern edition. The likely root cause is the known
numbering offset in this stretch of the series, where the newspaper numbering and
the later book numbering diverge by one.

**Resolution:** the script does not assert either date. It says the paper "ran in the
New York papers in the first days of January, seventeen eighty eight," and then says
plainly that "the sources actually disagree by a day about which morning it appeared."
This is a fair hedge, not invented certainty. ✅ as hedged.

### B2. ⚠️ Hamilton's Roman history is inaccurate — stated openly in the script

This is the episode's main honest-hedging moment, and it is deliberate.

Hamilton describes the two assemblies as "distinct and independent legislatures... each
having power to ANNUL or REPEAL the acts of the other."

Three problems, all named in the script:

1. **They were not two separate legislatures with separate constituencies.** Both were
   assemblies of the same Roman people, differently organized for counting votes.
2. **No power to annul or repeal each other's acts is attested.** A later law could
   supersede an earlier one, as in any legal system, but that is not two bodies
   vetoing one another. No source found supports Hamilton's framing.
3. **He conflates the comitia tributa with the concilium plebis.** The body in which
   the plebeian interest had genuinely total control was the plebeian council, which
   patricians could not join; it is that body whose resolutions bound all Romans after
   287 BC.

Verdict: ⚠️ **contested/incorrect in the source paper, and the script says so.** The
script states plainly that "Hamilton's history here is bent," explains each of the
three problems, notes that eighteenth-century writers did this routinely, and then
argues that the structural point survives without the annulment claim. This mirrors
the treatment of Plutarch/Aspasia in Episode 6. The script does **not** repeat
Hamilton's error as fact.

### B4. The British budget claim — Hamilton's arithmetic checked

Hamilton's claim: in Great Britain "not above a fifteenth part of the annual income of
the nation is appropriated to" civil government; "the other fourteen fifteenths are
absorbed in the payment of the interest of debts... and in the maintenance of fleets
and armies."

| Claim | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| UK central government spending in 1788 ≈ £16.3 million | ✅ | https://www.ukpublicspending.co.uk/uk_year1788_0.html (derived from B. R. Mitchell, *British Historical Statistics*) |
| Interest on debt ≈ £9.4 million (57.7%) | ✅ | Same |
| Defence ≈ £4.4 million (27.0%) | ✅ | Same |
| General government ≈ £1.2 million (7.4%) | ✅ | Same |
| Therefore Hamilton's "one fifteenth" (6.67%) is close to the actual 7.4% | ✅ | Arithmetic |

The script says Hamilton was "right to within a rounding error." That is fair: 7.4%
versus his 6.7%. Note the script phrases his claim as a share of **what the government
spends each year**, which is the faithful reading of his "annual income of the nation"
given his own "fourteen fifteenths" breakdown. ✅

Two things the script is careful *not* to claim: it does not present the ~£16.3m
reconstruction as a figure Hamilton had, and it does not claim his 14/15ths is exact
(actual debt + defence = 84.7%, with a further 8.6% unallocated "balance").

## Summary

- **Quote checks: 14 / 14 pass, 0 fail** (mechanical, normalized substring match).
- **External claims checked: 47 — 44 ✅ verified, 3 ⚠️ fairly hedged, 0 ❌ wrong.**

The three hedged items are:
1. The publication date (Avalon says 4 January, Founders Online says 5 January) — the
   script asserts neither and says the sources disagree.
2. Hamilton's account of the Roman assemblies, which is inaccurate — the script says so
   at length and explains exactly how, rather than repeating it.
3. The 1788-to-today currency conversion, given only as an order of magnitude with an
   explicit warning that precision here is not achievable.

No claim required correction during this pass, because the load-bearing historical
material (the Roman assemblies, the Dutch crisis, the British budget figures, and the
modern budget shares) was researched and verified **before** drafting rather than after.
Consequently there were no numbers, names or dates to sweep for repeat occurrences.

Two claims were considered and deliberately left out:
- That France's failure in the Dutch crisis "caused" the French Revolution. The fiscal
  crisis and the Assembly of Notables are stated as contemporaneous fact; no causal
  claim is made, because the causation is contested.
- A precise dollar figure for Hamilton's £200,000 forecast. See item 3 above.
