# Federalist No. 34 Explained: The Bill You Can't See Coming

## Episode Description
Eighty five essays, and this is the one where Hamilton makes a forecast. A real one, about the far future, with a number attached, in writing, where anybody can check it. He tells you what American government will cost, which of the two governments will do the expensive part, and what the other one should need each year. Two hundred and thirty eight years later we get to grade his work, and the grade is strange: he was wrong about the number by orders of magnitude, and right about the rule in a way the country may not have survived without. Along the way, two Roman assemblies that shared a city for centuries and prove the whole objection impossible, a storm gathering over Europe that his readers could name from their newspapers, and the moment the most powerful monarchy in Europe was caught bluffing because it could not afford the war it threatened.

## Short Episode Description
A man in 1788 wrote down his prediction for what American government would cost, and we can finally check his math. He missed by a factor you would not believe, and that turns out to be the best argument he ever made.

## Alternate Short Description
Every constitution is a bet about a future nobody can see, and this is the one essay where Hamilton shows his arithmetic.
