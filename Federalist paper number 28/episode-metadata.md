# Federalist No. 28 Explained: The People Hold the Balance

## Episode Description
Hamilton finally says the thing his critics were sure he would never admit: yes, once in a great while, even a free government has to answer force with force, because rebellions and insurrections happen in every society that has ever existed. Then he turns that honest admission into the strongest argument for the union in the whole debate. Because the very thing the critics feared, a government strong enough to use force, is also, when it is built the American way, a government that can never turn that force into lasting tyranny. This episode follows how two governments watching each other, resting on one people who can always tip the balance, in a country far too large for any affordable army to conquer, become the deepest safeguard of American liberty, and why that safeguard is not a promise on parchment but the people themselves.

## Short Episode Description
Hamilton admits a free government will sometimes need force, then shows why the people, armed with their own state governments, can never be permanently ruled by it.

## Alternate Short Description
The critics feared a government strong enough to use force; Hamilton reveals why, in a large union of watchful states, the people always hold the final say.
