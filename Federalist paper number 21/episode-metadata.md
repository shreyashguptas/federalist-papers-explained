# Federalist No. 21 Explained: A Government With No Teeth

## Episode Description
For three papers the founders toured a graveyard of foreign confederacies; now Hamilton turns the lamp on America's own government and starts naming, one by one, exactly what was broken in the Articles of Confederation. He lands on three crippling flaws: a government with no power to make anyone obey it, a union with no power to stop one of its own states from being toppled by force, and a money system so unfair it tried to measure a state's wealth by counting its acres. Along the way he points to Shays' Rebellion and asks the chilling question of what would have happened if those desperate farmers had been led by a Caesar, and he closes with a strikingly modern idea: a tax on the things people buy quietly polices itself, because pushed too high it simply stops paying. It is the first honest diagnosis of the patient in the room, and the first glimpse of the cure the Constitution was built to be.

## Short Episode Description
Hamilton stops examining dead foreign governments and turns on America's own, naming three fatal flaws in the Articles of Confederation, and the surprising fix hiding inside a tax.

## Alternate Short Description
A government that could only ask, never command, that had to watch as a state was nearly torn apart, and that tried to split its bills by counting farmland, and why all three failures point to the same cure.
