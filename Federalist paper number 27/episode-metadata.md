# Federalist No. 27 Explained: Why Good Government Needs No Army

## Episode Description
The critics of the Constitution had a stark warning: a national government this powerful could never simply be obeyed. It would have to be enforced at the point of a bayonet, an army standing behind every tax and every law. In Federalist No. 27, Hamilton turns that fear on its head. A government that is competently run and woven into the ordinary business of everyday life, he argues, earns obedience the way any good institution does, and it enforces its laws through the courts and sworn officials that every state already has, not through soldiers. This episode traces his surprising claim that closeness breeds loyalty rather than resistance, and that force is the mark of a government that has failed, not the foundation of a strong one.

## Short Episode Description
Hamilton's answer to the fear that the new government could only rule by force: a government that is good at its job, and part of daily life, is obeyed without an army.

## Alternate Short Description
Why the critics were wrong that a powerful national government would need soldiers to be obeyed, and how the Constitution enforces its laws through courts instead of camps.
