# Federalist No. 36 Explained: The Power He Hoped Nobody Would Use

## Episode Description
Every objection Hamilton answers in this essay has the same shape: they could do something terrible. His replies never once ask you to trust anybody's character. Instead he asks what the terrible thing would actually cost a government, and what it would gain, which turns the whole argument from intentions into incentives. This episode follows that move through three tests history ran on it: the tax revolt that broke out ten years later when federal assessors showed up to count people's windows, the day Hamilton stood before the Supreme Court and argued away his own safeguard, and the tax he said he hated and refused to give up anyway.

## Short Episode Description
Hamilton admits he dislikes the poll tax, and then explains why he still will not let anyone ban it.

## Alternate Short Description
The last of seven essays on money, and the one where he argues a power does its best work by never being used.
