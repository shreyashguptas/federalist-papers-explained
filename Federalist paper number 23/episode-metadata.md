# Federalist No. 23 Explained: Strong Enough to Defend Us

## Episode Description
The tearing-down is over, and the building begins. Having spent twenty-two essays explaining why America's first government failed, Hamilton turns to the new one and starts with its rawest question: how much power should it have? His answer is bold enough to alarm people who had just fought a war against too much power. A government responsible for defending a nation, he argues, must be given strength to match the job, with no limits set in advance, because no one can foresee what dangers will come. This episode follows that argument through the night he can't forget — the starving, barefoot Continental Army at Valley Forge, dying not from British bullets but from a government too weak to feed it — and out to the long shadow it casts over every modern fight about national security and freedom. The means, Hamilton insists, must always match the end. And the bigger the country, the stronger the government it takes to hold it together.

## Short Episode Description
Hamilton starts building the case for the new government and lands on a bold first principle: a government meant to defend a nation must be strong enough to actually do it, with no limits written in advance.

Why the man who watched an army starve at Valley Forge believed you can never put a leash on the power to keep a nation safe.
