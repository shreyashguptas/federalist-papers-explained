# Federalist No. 22 Explained: We the People, Not the States

## Episode Description
The diagnosis of America's first government gets its final, devastating chapter. Hamilton piles on the defects that finish off the Articles of Confederation: a government that cannot control trade while the states quietly tax each other into enemies, an army raised by a bidding war for soldiers, a voting rule that lets a handful of tiny states freeze the entire country, a wide-open door to foreign bribery, and no national courts to make the law mean one thing. From the rivers of Germany choked by tolls, to the slow death of Poland, to a Swedish king who became absolute in a single day, the warnings are vivid and real. And then the list stops being a list and becomes a verdict — this government cannot be repaired, only replaced, and the replacement must draw its power not from a deal among state legislatures, but straight from the people, the idea that would become the first three words of the Constitution.

## Short Episode Description
Hamilton finishes tearing down America's first government — trade chaos, a paralyzing minority veto, foreign bribery, no courts — and lands on the idea that would open the Constitution: power must come from the people, not the states.

## Alternate Short Description
Why a government built as a treaty among states was doomed to fail, and why the fix turned out to be three words: We the People.
