# Federalist Podcast Brand System

## Goal
Create a podcast brand for simple, clean, modern explanations of the Federalist Papers.

The brand should feel:
- clear
- trustworthy
- historical but not dusty
- smart but accessible
- searchable

## Show Name
**The Federalist Papers: Explained**

Why:
- best SEO/search match
- obvious to new listeners
- scales cleanly to a long series
- works well in podcast apps

## Subtitle / short show description
- `Simple, modern explainers for every Federalist Paper.`
- `A plain-English guide to the Federalist Papers.`
- `Clear, short explainers of the essays that shaped the Constitution.`

## Show description
`The Federalist Papers, Explained is a plain-English podcast that walks through the essays that shaped the U.S. Constitution. Each episode breaks down one paper, what it argued, why it mattered, and what it still means today.`

## Episode title system

### Default title formula (locked in)
`Federalist No. {N} Explained: {short topic}`

Examples:
- `Federalist No. 1 Explained: Why These Essays Matter`
- `Federalist No. 2 Explained: Union as Safety`
- `Federalist No. 3 Explained: Why Union Prevents War`

### Alternate shorter formula
Use when brevity matters:
`Federalist No. {N}: {short topic}`

## Topic line guidance
Keep the topic line:
- under 6 words when possible
- plain English
- concept-first, not academic

Good examples:
- `Why These Essays Matter`
- `Union as Safety`
- `The Danger of Division`
- `Why Strong Government Matters`

Avoid:
- vague poetic phrases
- overly legal titles
- subtitles that require prior history knowledge

## Episode description template

Use this structure:
`Written by {author}, Federalist No. {N} argues that {core argument in plain English}. In this episode, we break down what the essay was trying to persuade the public of, why it mattered at the founding, and what it can still teach us today.`

Optional longer format:
`Federalist No. {N} argues that {core argument in plain English}. This episode explains the essay in simple language, the historical problem it was responding to, and the main takeaway a modern listener should understand.`

## Metadata checklist per episode
For each episode, produce:
- episode number
- canonical title
- short topic line
- 1-2 sentence description
- 3-5 keywords or tags
- art variant text: `Episode {N}`

## Script-writing rules

> The full, enforced rubric lives in `episode-production-workflow.md` (the Gate sequence). This is the summary. Where they overlap, the workflow doc is authoritative. **The fixed gold standard for voice and structure is Episodes 5, 6, and 7.**

When writing a full podcast episode script for this series:
- write for a single warm host voice, not a classroom narrator
- **tell stories *about* the paper's argument — never walk through the paper paragraph by paragraph.** An annotated read-through is the series' biggest failure mode (it crept in at Episode 8 and took over by 9–10).
- **structure the episode independently of the paper's paragraph order** — the host decides what to teach first, not the source text
- target a **25 to 40 minute** final spoken runtime, landing in the lower-middle of that band; past 38 minutes is a red flag for exegesis, not a sign of a deep paper
- do not produce a short polished summary when a full explainer is needed
- make the episode stand on its own for a first-time listener
- explain historical context plainly without sounding childish
- use **4 to 8 substantial exact quotes per episode, no more** — only the lines where listeners genuinely benefit from the original language; paraphrase in the host's voice is unlimited and is how you cover the rest
- explain each quote immediately in simple language
- avoid quote-dump structure — and note that many small quotes, each explained, is still a quote dump
- include **2–3 story set-pieces** per episode where the script slows down and tells something richly
- prefer a flowing narrative with strong transitions
- avoid formatting patterns that create long dead pauses in TTS
- **convey the paper's spine** — its 3–4 load-bearing ideas — richly, so a first-time listener can follow the whole argument; "covered" does *not* mean every paragraph and every quotable line made it in

### Listener-question rule
In each episode, deliberately include a few natural moments that answer the obvious question a listener may be quietly asking.

Use moves like:
- `At this point, you might be wondering...`
- `So why does that matter?`
- `A fair question here is...`
- `Now you might ask...`

Use these sparingly, but on purpose. Usually 3 to 6 of these moments per episode is enough.

### Series-promise rule for Episode 1
In Episode 1, near the beginning, briefly explain:
- who this series is for
- what listeners will get from the full show
- why a simple 85-episode walkthrough is useful

Keep this concise and welcoming.

### Continuity rule for Episodes 2-85
For later episodes, look for genuine factual links to earlier papers already covered.
When useful, briefly reference earlier episodes so the full series feels cumulative rather than isolated.
**Continuity is for *facts and callbacks* only. For *voice and structure*, always anchor on the fixed gold standard — Episodes 5, 6, and 7 — never on "the previous episode." Anchoring on N−1 lets small drift compound across the series.**

### Script quality bar
The final script should sound like:
- a smart, natural podcast
- a host who understands where the listener may get lost
- a conversation, not a lecture
- a storyteller telling you about the paper — not a reader walking you through it

### Accuracy rule
Factual accuracy is a hard requirement, not a style preference.
For any full episode script:
- draft first
- then run a dedicated fact-check pass
- revise anything inaccurate, overstated, weakly supported, or loosely paraphrased
- only approve the script for audio after it clears that fact-check pass

## Visual system

### Core look
- dark navy or parchment background
- cream / ivory text
- one accent color: muted gold
- strong serif headline or classic editorial look
- minimal visual clutter
- subtle constitutional / 18th century cues, not cheesy patriotic graphics

### Show art concept
- clean premium cover
- large title: `The Federalist Papers, Explained`
- subtitle optional: `Plain-English guides to the essays behind the Constitution`
- visual motif: parchment texture, engraved linework, quill, constitutional manuscript texture, or stylized capitol/dome seal
- square composition for podcast apps
- extremely legible at small size

### Episode art system
Use the same exact art system as the show art, with one change:
- add a bottom band, medallion, or small label with `Episode {N}` or `No. {N}`

Do not create fully different art for each episode unless explicitly requested.

## Prompt template: show art
`Create premium podcast cover art for a show called "The Federalist Papers, Explained." Style: elegant, historical, minimal, modern editorial. Use a deep navy or parchment background, cream typography, and muted gold accents. Include subtle references to the U.S. founding era such as parchment texture, engraved lines, constitutional manuscript motifs, or a restrained quill/archival design. Avoid cheesy flags, eagles, or crowded patriotic symbols. The title must be highly legible at thumbnail size. Square podcast cover, polished, timeless, intelligent, accessible.`

## Prompt template: episode art
`Create podcast episode cover art matching the visual identity of "The Federalist Papers, Explained." Use the same deep navy/parchment palette, cream typography, and muted gold accents. Keep the design consistent with the series cover. Add a clear episode marker reading "Federalist No. {N}" near the bottom. Maintain a premium editorial look with subtle parchment or engraved historical texture. Keep the artwork highly legible at small size and avoid clutter.`

## Episode art prompt rule
Keep the exact same show-art visual identity and generate per-episode art with only one extra line of text, **Federalist No. {N}**, with no subtitle, no topic line, no author name, and no extra badges or labels.
