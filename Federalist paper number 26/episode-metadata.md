# Federalist No. 26 Explained: What Really Guards Our Freedom

## Episode Description
If a written ban on a peacetime army cannot keep a free people safe, then what can? This episode follows Hamilton as he answers that question, drawing a lesson from England's own history: even at the high tide of its liberty, England did not outlaw armies, it simply forced them under the control of an elected legislature. The real safeguard built into the new Constitution, he argues, is not a wall of words but a clock, the rule that army funding must be voted again every two years, out in the open, by representatives who have to answer to the voters, and watched the whole time by a rival opposition party and by vigilant state governments. Freedom, in the end, is protected less by what a document forbids than by a people who keep paying attention.

## Short Episode Description
Hamilton reveals the real protection against a runaway military, and it is not a ban written on paper, it is a public vote the government is forced to take over and over, forever.

## Alternate Short Description
Why the true guardian of a free people is not a rule that outlaws armies, but a legislature that has to look its own soldiers in the eye and vote to pay for them every two years.
