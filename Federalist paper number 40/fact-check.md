### Three quote drifts caught and corrected

The mechanical check earned its place again. Three renderings had drifted from the source while still being presented as the author's words:

1. **"the most probable means"** — source reads *"the most probable **mean**"* (singular, eighteenth-century usage). Rather than force a singular that would sound like an error in audio, the sentence was recast so only the exact phrase *a firm national government* is quoted and the rest is plainly the host's paraphrase.
2. **"every part ought if possible to be allowed some meaning"** — dropped *"of the expression"*. **Restored to source wording.**
3. **"Conventions elected in the several states…"** — dropped *"were"*. **Restored to source wording.**

### Four attribution splices inside quotations — removed

In four places an attribution (*"he said,"* / *"they wrote,"*) had been inserted **inside** a quoted sentence, in one case replacing source words (*"and which could not"* → *"they wrote, could not"*). All four rewritten so the attribution sits outside the quotation and the quoted words run verbatim: **Yates and Lansing, Paterson, Wilson, and the May thirtieth resolution** (which had also acquired an inserted comma; removed).

### Two claims cut rather than hedged

Held to the same standard as the rest of the episode; neither could be sourced.

1. **"Before roughly a third of the delegates had even reached Philadelphia…"** — no reliable figure for how many delegates were physically present on 30 May 1787. Searches returned the convention totals (55 attended overall; quorum of seven states on 25 May) but no headcount for that day. **Cut entirely**; the point stands on the sitting-day count alone.
2. **Yates as the author of "Brutus"** — attractive, because Episode 39 quoted Brutus I and Yates is a leading candidate. But authorship is genuinely disputed (Melancton Smith is the other main candidate). **Left out** rather than asserted or hedged.

### Where the episode argues against Madison

Recorded here because these are the episode's own judgments, not sourced claims:

- The lawyer's argument requires a **preamble's statement of hopes to override an operative clause's statement of limits**, which is the reverse of how such conflicts are normally resolved.
- The "fundamental principles" move finds the **smallest existing version of each power and uses it to license the largest proposed version** — honest, but still a difference-in-degree/difference-in-kind problem.
- Randolph's and Madison's necessity argument is described as **"a loaded gun"**: entirely self-certifying, since whoever judges the crisis severe enough is the same person who wants to act.
- The episode states plainly that the convention **had an in-mandate alternative** (the New Jersey Plan) and **voted it down seven to three** — a fact Federalist 40 never mentions.
- The transmittal's **"Resolved unanimously"** is flagged as misleading in effect, even though literally true about the act of forwarding.
