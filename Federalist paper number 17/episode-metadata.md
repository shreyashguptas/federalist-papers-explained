# Federalist No. 17 Explained: The Government You Can Feel

## Episode Description
Once Hamilton convinced people the new government could reach every citizen directly, his opponents had a fresh fear: a central power that strong would grow until it swallowed the states whole. Federalist No. 17 is his answer, and it is a surprising one — he says the danger runs the other way. The states, not the union, will always hold the upper hand, because the states run the part of government people actually see and feel: the everyday courts, the local justice, the daily business of ordinary life. Hamilton illustrates it with the warlords of medieval Europe, and along the way he quietly predicts exactly how a distant federal government could one day win the people's loyalty after all — which is roughly the story of the last hundred years.

## Short Episode Description
Won't a strong central government swallow the states? Hamilton says no — and explains why you're loyal to the government closest to home.

## Alternate Short Description
The government that judges your disputes and protects your property is the one you trust — and in 1787 that was your state, not Washington.
