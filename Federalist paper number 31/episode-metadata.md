# Federalist No. 31 Explained: The Rules of the Argument

## Episode Description
Handed the sharpest objection of the whole ratification fight, that a government free to tax anything would quietly starve the states out of existence, Hamilton answers by talking about geometry. This episode follows that strange move and shows why it was the right one: some claims in politics are close enough to self-evident that you have to argue with them rather than around them, the reason people cannot see them usually sits inside the person rather than the question, and if imagining an abuse counts as winning an argument then nothing can ever be built. Along the way, an anonymous rival writing in a competing New York newspaper, a lawyer who taught himself Euclid to find out what proof means, and a modern experiment on why smart people get worse at math when the numbers threaten their politics.

## Short Episode Description
Faced with the strongest argument against the Constitution, Hamilton stops to settle something first: what is even going to count as a fair answer.

## Alternate Short Description
Why the founding's most practical fight about money turned into an argument about self-evident truths, motivated reasoning, and where the real protection against abuse of power actually lives.
