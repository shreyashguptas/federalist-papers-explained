### Internal-consistency sweep (script against itself)

Every count and date asserted in the script was extracted and cross-checked before any external research:

- **"Exactly three ways" a power becomes exclusive** → enumerated first / second / third, then each given an example (seat of government, imports and exports, naturalization), then all three re-applied to taxation. Consistent; no fourth case appears anywhere.
- **"You have just learned two things"** in the house-rule passage → "The first is obvious… But the second thing…", followed by an explicitly labelled "third thing hiding in there". The count of two is stated before the third is introduced as an addition, not as part of the pair. Consistent.
- **Brutus's clauses: "those three"** → preceded by first / second / third. Consistent.
- **Ratification count** → "three states had ratified" on New Year's Day; "Georgia became the fourth" on 2 January. Consistent with each other and with Ep. 31.
- **"Thirty one years"** → asserted three times (the teaser, the McCulloch section, the "never been denied" beat). 1788 to 1819 = 31. All three agree.
- **"Sixteen hundred words"** → asserted three times ("under sixteen hundred words", "about sixteen hundred words", "a sixteen hundred word newspaper essay"). Source file is 1,514 words including its four header lines, so the essay body is roughly 1,480. All three phrasings agree and are correctly hedged.
- **Convention vote tallies** → "five to six" (Madison's two-thirds motion) and "seven to four" (the ban). The seven named ayes and four named noes match those totals exactly.
- **Publication dates** → "second of January" (Independent Journal) stated at the open and again at the close for Numbers 32 and 33 together; "the following day" for the Daily Advertiser. Consistent.

Two internal errors were corrected during this sweep:

- Changed "That is the whole thing, in **three** words" → "in **four** words." The phrase being counted is "which they before had," which is four words. Swept the script for every other asserted word-count: **1 other found** (the "sixteen hundred words" family, verified above); **0 remaining.**
- Changed "**Two hundred years** of American federalism are sitting inside it" → "The whole modern architecture of American federalism is sitting inside it." 1788 to 2026 is 238 years, so "two hundred years" understated it and invited the same arithmetic objection corrected in Episode 31. Swept for every other elapsed-time figure: **2 others found** ("thirty one years", verified exact; "three years later" for the Tenth Amendment, 1788 to 1791, verified exact); **0 remaining.**

Two external errors were also corrected during this pass and are logged here because they changed distinctive wording:

- Changed "a bank **teller** in Baltimore" → "a bank **officer** in Baltimore." James McCulloch was the *cashier* of the Baltimore branch, which in the period meant the branch's chief operating officer, not a counter clerk. Swept for every other description of McCulloch: **1 other found** ("James McCulloch was the cashier of the Baltimore branch"), which was already correct; **0 remaining.**
- Rewrote the Number 33 teaser. The draft said Hamilton calls the necessary-and-proper and supremacy clauses "tautologies." He does not assert that; he concedes that the *declaration* "may be chargeable with tautology or redundancy" and argues the clauses are "only declaratory of a truth" that would have followed anyway. The teaser now states the declaratory claim and reports the redundancy concession as a concession. Swept for any other characterization of No. 33: **0 further instances found; 0 remaining.**

### Modern parallels — distortion check

- **The pay stub / two withholding lines** — used to show that concurrent taxation is real and unwritten, which is exactly the paper's claim. Does not overstate: the script says the arrangement was argued into existence, not that this essay is legally binding.
- **The Tenth Amendment** — presented as vindicating Hamilton's default rule *and* as a defeat for his position that it needed no writing. Both sides scored honestly.
- **Expressio unius in modern courts** — presented as the same technique, not as derived from Hamilton. Correct: he says himself it is "what lawyers call" it.
- **Ninety percent tariff revenue** — used to concede that Brutus had a point about the union taking the best revenue first, before noting the outcome went a different way. Does not stack the deck.
- **McCulloch v. Maryland** — used to say Hamilton was **incomplete**, not wrong, and the script states plainly which half survived and which did not. Marshall's endorsement of the default rule and his limit on it are both reported.
- **Sixteenth Amendment and modern state revenue** — used to say that *neither* Hamilton nor Brutus predicted the mechanism. Symmetric treatment.
