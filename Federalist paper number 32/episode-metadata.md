# Federalist No. 32 Explained: What the States Kept

## Episode Description
Look at a pay stub and you will find two governments taking a share of the same dollar on the same day, neither one asking the other's permission. That arrangement is not written anywhere in the Constitution. It was argued into existence in about sixteen hundred words, in a newspaper, by a man answering a rival columnist, and this episode follows how he did it. Along the way, a rule about reading that lawyers still use every day and that you already use without noticing, a hot room in Philadelphia in August of 1787 where the one exception was bargained into the document, and a bank officer in Baltimore who thirty one years later found the one place the argument did not quite hold.

## Short Episode Description
The reason your paycheck has two tax lines instead of one was decided by an argument about a sentence, in a newspaper, in 1788.

## Alternate Short Description
How Hamilton proved that the states keep every power they never gave away, by teaching his readers that what a document does not say is evidence too.
