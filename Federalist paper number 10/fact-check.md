### Note on the modern-democracy parallel (claim 13)
The one passage warranting an author's note. The genuine scholarly caution (Douglass Adair; the fact that Madison defined "republic" differently in Federalist 39) is that Madison's technical "republic" should not be flattened into "he was just describing modern democracy." **The script avoids that error** because it (i) keeps the pure-democracy vs. republic distinction sharp throughout, and (ii) attaches the "brake, not a guarantee" caveat, explicitly acknowledging that tyrannical majorities have in fact spanned the whole country for generations. The single strongest reach is the line "nearly every large democracy on earth runs, knowingly or not, on some version of it" — it stays on the right side of the line *only* because of the paired caveat. Verdict: defensible interpretation, not distortion. If either guardrail were removed in a future edit, this would slide toward overreach.

Note also: the script makes **no claim** that Fed 10 was obscure/unrecognized until a 20th-century (Beard) revival — so there was no such dated-fame claim to verify. It says only that "many people consider" it the best essay and that it is "the most famous," which is uncontroversial.
