**⚠️ Minor nuance (not an error):** In illustrating Hamilton's sweeping phrase "the petty republics of Greece and Italy," the script lists "Florence, Venice, Genoa, Milan" as "little city-states." Strictly, Renaissance Milan was a duchy (a monarchy) for most of the period, and Venice was famously one of the *more* stable republics (the "myth of Venice"), not a byword for anarchy. This does not distort the argument: the "horror and disgust" verdict is explicitly framed as *Hamilton's* rhetoric ("his verdict," "he says"), the names are offered as representative examples of small city-states, and Hamilton's broader point — that small, self-standing republics historically proved prone to faction — is well supported.
