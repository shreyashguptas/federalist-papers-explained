# Federalist No. 18 Explained: What Ancient Greece Got Wrong

## Episode Description
Ancient Greece tried, twice, to bind its proud city-states into a single union — and both times it ended in chains. Hamilton and Madison open the history books and lay the two great Greek confederacies on the table like an autopsy. The first, the Amphictyonic Council, had more power on paper than America's own failing government, and even claimed the gods at Delphi on its side — yet it collapsed into bullying and was handed to Philip of Macedon by the very cities too jealous to unite, a "friend" who was invited in and stayed as a master. The second, the Achaean League, was a tighter, wiser union that actually gave its members calmer, fairer government than they ever managed alone — before it, too, was flattered and bribed apart, this time by Rome. And the verdict the founders pull from the rubble is the one the whole Constitution was built to answer: a confederacy dies not from a center grown too strong, but from squabbling members and a center too weak to hold them.

## Short Episode Description
Twice, ancient Greece tried to unite its city-states — and twice it fell apart; here's the autopsy the founders read so they wouldn't repeat it.

## Alternate Short Description
A union can look all-powerful on paper and be helpless in fact — the story of how a divided Greece kept inviting in "friends" who became its masters.
