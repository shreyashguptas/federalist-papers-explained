# Federalist No. 20 Explained: When the Sword Is All You Have

## Episode Description
For five papers now, the founders have toured the wreckage of history's confederacies — and now they reach the one that hurts: the Dutch republic, the rich, free, Protestant union that Americans most admired and tried to imitate. On paper it looked like a real government; in practice it was so paralyzed that one wealthy province quietly paid everyone's bills and then collected the unpaid shares from its neighbors at the point of a bayonet, a hereditary prince was the only thing holding the whole thing together, and — at the very moment these words were being written — a foreign army was marching through its streets. Out of that wreckage Madison delivers the verdict the whole stretch of history was building toward: a government that rules other governments instead of people can enforce itself only with the sword, never with the quiet machinery of ordinary law. It is the climax of the case against the old order, and the hinge on which the founders finally turn to fixing their own.

## Short Episode Description
The founders examine the one republic Americans loved most — the Dutch — find it dying of the very same disease as all the rest, and deliver the hard verdict the whole series has been building toward.

## Alternate Short Description
A union so broken it collected taxes from its own members at gunpoint and needed a hereditary prince just to function — and why it is the last warning the founders give before they finally turn to fixing their own government.
