# Federalist No. 29 Explained: The Citizen in Arms

## Episode Description
The critics had a fresh fear: the new Constitution would let the federal government organize, arm, and call up the militia, and to them that was a second weapon aimed at the people, stacked on top of the standing army they already dreaded. Hamilton turns the fear completely inside out. A well-regulated militia, he argues, is not a threat sitting beside a standing army; it is the very thing that makes a standing army unnecessary. This episode walks through what a militia actually was in early America, why you cannot afford to drill an entire nation into soldiers, why Hamilton wanted a small, genuinely trained "select corps" instead, and how splitting control (national standards, state officers) keeps the armed citizen answerable to home. The payoff is the quiet climax of the whole defense arc: the ultimate guard of a free people was never a promise on paper. It was the people themselves, armed, and standing as their own counterweight.

## Short Episode Description
Hamilton explains why an armed citizenry, your own neighbors and sons, is not a danger to a free country but the reason it can stay free without a standing army.

## Alternate Short Description
Who controls the militia, why you cannot turn a whole nation into soldiers, and how the citizen with a musket became liberty's last line of defense.
