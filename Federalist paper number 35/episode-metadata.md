# Federalist No. 35 Explained: Nobody in Congress Looks Like You

## Episode Description
Hamilton spends half of this essay on a technical question about tariffs and the other half on an uncomfortable one about who gets elected, and almost everyone treats them as two separate arguments. They are the same argument. A tax has a name on it and a different person actually pays it. A representative has a background and a different thing entirely ties him to you. This episode follows both halves through three stories: John Hancock's smuggled wine and the fleet Hamilton later built to stop people like him, a warship named for Hamilton hauled through Manhattan by the very tradesmen he said would never send one of their own to Congress, and the New York tenant farmers who went to war with their landlord and broke the one claim in this paper Hamilton was most certain about.

## Short Episode Description
Hamilton argues that working people will never elect working people, and two hundred and thirty eight years of data say he was right.

## Alternate Short Description
The person named on a tax is rarely the person who pays it, and Hamilton thought the same thing was true of your representative.
