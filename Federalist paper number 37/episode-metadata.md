# Federalist No. 37 Explained: Why Nobody Could Write It Perfectly

## Episode Description
Madison takes over the series and opens the strangest way a campaign document can open: by telling you the Constitution is flawed. Not flawed in some small fixable way, but flawed on principle, because the things a government has to describe do not have sharp edges, the human mind is a limited instrument, and words leak meaning no matter how carefully they are chosen. His remedy is not better drafting. It is time. A law is not finished the day it is written; it gets settled later, one hard case at a time. This episode follows the man who never missed a day in that sealed Philadelphia room, the single word of his that became a doctrine the Supreme Court still reaches for, and the afternoon the convention would not even vote on praying.

## Short Episode Description
The man who took the notes at the Constitutional Convention explains why no constitution could ever come out clear, and why that is good news.

## Alternate Short Description
Madison argues that even God's meaning would go cloudy in human language, so nobody should be shocked that the Constitution did.
