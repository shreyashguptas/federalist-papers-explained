# Federalist No. 39 Explained: Neither One Nor the Other

## Episode Description
Madison's opponents came at him with a yes or no question that had a trap in it: is this a federal government or a national one? Say federal and you are lying about a government that reaches past the states to tax people directly. Say national and you have admitted to swallowing the states whole. Madison refuses the question, picks the Constitution up, and looks at it from five different sides, and the answer changes depending on which side you are looking at. This episode also asks what nobody asks about this paper: whether the definition of a republic he invents here is loose enough to mean anything, and why the sentence he lands on was so accurate that both sides of the argument were still quoting it eighty years later, with a war in between.

## Short Episode Description
Is this government federal or national? Madison's answer was that it is both, and it was so accurate that it settled nothing for the next eighty years.

## Alternate Short Description
The word republic had gone useless, so Madison wrote his own definition, and then took the Constitution apart seam by seam to prove it fit.
