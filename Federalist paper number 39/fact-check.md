### A2. Quote drift caught and restored

**One quote had drifted and was restored rather than shipped as a disclosed modification.**

- **Federalist 15 (Hamilton), the "great and radical vice" line.** Drafted as *"the great and radical vice of the Articles was legislating for states or governments in their corporate or collective capacities, as contradistinguished from the individuals of which they consist."* The source reads *"The great and radical vice in the construction of the existing Confederation is in the principle of **LEGISLATION** for STATES or GOVERNMENTS, in their CORPORATE or COLLECTIVE CAPACITIES, **and** as contradistinguished from the INDIVIDUALS of which they consist."* Two changes: "legislation" had become "legislating", and "and" was dropped. **Restored to the source wording.** ✅

All 20 Federalist 39 quotes passed on the first mechanical check — no drift in the paper's own text.

## The four ⚠️ hedges, all stated honestly in the script or handled by removal

1. **Patrick Henry's share of the debate** — the unsourceable "one third of floor time" claim was cut; the script now says only that he spoke more than anyone else in the room, day after day, which the sources support.
2. **"Centinel"** — removed rather than asserted, and replaced with two sources verified to carry the consolidation charge before January 1788 (Brutus I, and the Pennsylvania minority's dissent).
3. **Virginia's senate term** — the state-by-state term comparison is Madison's driest passage and is summarized in the host's voice rather than enumerated, precisely because one figure in it rests on inference from a rotation clause rather than an explicit term.
4. **Civil War deaths** — the traditional 620,000 is given as "something like six hundred thousand… and by some modern counts a good many more," rather than stated as settled.

## Where the episode argues against Madison

Recorded here because it is deliberate, not an oversight.

- **The definition is built to fit.** The script points out plainly that this is *not* the definition of a republic Madison gave in Federalist 10 two months earlier, where the word was defined by representation as against pure democracy. Here it is defined by source-of-power and tenure. Same man, same year, different definitions, because each one is doing a different job.
- **"Indirectly" carries an enormous load.** Under Madison's test a life-tenured judge appointed by an indirectly elected president and confirmed by indirectly elected senators is still republican. The episode asks whether a definition that elastic is worth anything, and answers honestly: its value is not what it admits but the one thing it excludes, which is heredity.
- **The unanimity claim is thin.** Madison rests the "federal foundation" on the *unanimous* assent of the states, but Article VII required only nine. The script names this as a crack, and then shows Madison conceding it four papers later in Federalist 43.
- **The referee works for the other team.** Madison admits boundary disputes go to a federal court and argues it does not change the principle. The script credits the argument and notes why states have never found it fully comfortable.
- **The formula settled nothing.** The closing argument is that the accuracy of "a composition of both" is exactly why it armed both sides for eighty years, and that Madison spent his last years losing an argument to people quoting him.
