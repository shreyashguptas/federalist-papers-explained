# Federalist No. 30 Explained: The Power of the Purse

## Episode Description
Money, one founder argued, is the lifeblood of a government, the thing that keeps it alive and lets it act, and a country that cannot reliably raise it faces only two futures: it plunders its own people, or it slowly dies. This episode follows that argument as it takes apart the young republic's broken habit of begging the states for funds, explains why letting the national government tax only imports was a trap, and lands on the hard truth at the center of the paper: a government that cannot tax cannot borrow, and a government that cannot borrow cannot survive its first war. Along the way, an all-powerful ruler who was forbidden to tax, a Congress chased out of its own capital by unpaid soldiers, and a modern credit downgrade all turn out to be telling the same story, that the power of the purse is really the power to be believed.

## Short Episode Description
Why a government that cannot raise its own money is a government waiting to fail, and why the power to tax turns out to be the power to survive.

## Alternate Short Description
A ledger can be as dangerous as an army: the founding case for letting the government fund itself, and what happens to the one that cannot.
